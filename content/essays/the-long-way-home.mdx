There are two ways home from the station. One is eleven minutes and takes me past
the bins behind the pharmacy. The other is twenty-five, and goes along the canal,
where for about four hundred metres nothing is lit at all.

## Two ways

For years I took the short one on the grounds that it was shorter, which is a
very hard argument to beat. It is also, I have noticed, the argument I use for
every other choice of the same shape: fewer steps, less friction, more of the day
left over for the things I actually want to do.

The trouble is that I never spend the saved time on the things I actually want to
do. I spend it on the next decision of the same kind. Fourteen minutes saved is
fourteen minutes closer to the following fourteen minutes, and the ledger never
closes.

## What the darkness is for

The canal at night is not beautiful, exactly. The water is black and there is
usually a smell of something. But four hundred metres of unlit towpath is enough
time for a thought to arrive and be finished before I get to the door, which does
not happen at home, where there is a lamp and a screen and a list.

I am told this is not efficient. I agree. I have stopped treating that as an
objection, because efficiency is a claim about the destination, and I no longer
believe the destination is where the value is.

## Choosing badly on purpose

So I take the long way, most nights, knowing it is the worse choice by the only
measure I used to apply. It is a small refusal, and it costs nothing, and I think
the willingness to make small refusals is the whole of a life — the rest is
momentum and weather.

The bins behind the pharmacy will still be there tomorrow, if I change my mind.
