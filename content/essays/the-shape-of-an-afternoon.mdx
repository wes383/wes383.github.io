Most afternoons disappear. I don't mean that they pass quickly — I mean that
afterward I can't say what happened in them. The morning has a shape: coffee, the
first hour of work, the walk out to the corner and back. The evening has one too.
The middle is a long room with no furniture in it.

## Edges

I have started giving the afternoon small edges. Not appointments — those make
their own noise, and I spend the hour before them waiting. Just markers. A
different chair. Tea instead of coffee. Twenty minutes outside at three o'clock,
whether or not there is anything to see.

None of it is productive, which is the point. An edge is not there to make the
time useful. It is there so that the time has a *where*.

## The part I got wrong

For a while I thought the problem was discipline: that if I planned the afternoon
properly it would stop leaking. It didn't, and I think I know why now. A plan
tries to fill the hours; an edge only divides them. The difference is the one
between a shelf that is full and a shelf with a few things on it — both are
shelves, but only the second one lets you see the wood.

## October

The light helps. From about four the room changes colour and I notice I am in it,
which is all I wanted to begin with. Everything else I have tried has been an
attempt to buy that with apparatus.

The afternoon doesn't need a better plan. It needs a few objects, and a window.
